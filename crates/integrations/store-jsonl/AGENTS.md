The journal takes no file locks, following pi-durable, whose storage needs none. The single O_APPEND journal is the linearization point instead, which also keeps owner fencing across processes. A writer replays the tail, validates against it and appends its entries with one write call and one full disk sync. The write offset comes from the file position after an append-mode write. It then replays every entry that landed before its own and judges its own entries by the rule every reader applies. An acquire is valid when its fence is the next fence. A replacement is valid when its owner is the current owner and its revision is the next revision. Stale fences, revisions or owners are superseded: every reader skips them, and the writer reports Fenced or Conflict for a replacement, retries an acquisition, or retries acquire_with with the replacement its update already computed while the state revision is unchanged. A fence, revision or owner fence beyond the next one cannot come from a lagging writer and is corruption that fails replay. Multi-entry appends carry a batch id and count on every line and apply all or nothing; a batch interrupted by another line or a torn write is superseded, and a batch still incomplete at the end of the visible journal is left for the next refresh. Concurrent openers without a sidecar replay in parallel instead of queueing behind a lock. output/durability-lockfree-20261018 holds the multi-process harness: eight processes contending for one state with acquisitions every third round committed exactly the revisions they acknowledged while 121 of 303 lines were superseded, with and without concurrent compactions, after SIGKILLed writers and after a torn 24 MiB append. Eight writers making 64 fsynced 16 KiB replacements each took a median 2.48 s instead of 3.17 s with the previous process lock, and 3.21 s instead of 4.61 s with a compaction every 16 rounds.

A writer whose journal tail lacks a final newline, left by a writer killed during its append, starts its own append with a newline, so the torn fragment becomes one unparseable line that readers skip with a warning. Readers never truncate the journal and stop before an incomplete final line, because another process may still be writing it. Every process sharing a journal must run this protocol: older binaries lock, truncate tails they consider torn and reject superseded and seal lines, so restart every writer after upgrading.

StateStore::commit_many validates every replacement of the group against the replayed tail, appends the survivors as independent lines without a batch id in one write call with one full disk sync, then judges each line by the reader rule in order. A superseded line reports Fenced or Conflict like replace, a sealed group waits and retries, and the other commits keep their own outcomes; commits carrying events are NotCommitted. Readers therefore replay each line on its own, and an interrupted group leaves a prefix of independently valid lines.

StateStore::acquire_with evaluates its pure update once and writes the acquire and replace JSONL entries as one batch with one full disk sync before returning. A torn batch is superseded as a whole. JsonlStore is a handle to a dedicated worker thread that owns the journal, index and read cache. Each StateStore call sends one complete transaction as a job, so tail replay, record reads, F_FULLFSYNC, seal polling and index saves never run on an asynchronous runtime worker. The job channel holds one command: StateStore takes &mut self, so a handle has at most one live call, and the slot lets that caller queue its next command behind a cancelled predecessor that is still running; further sends wait for the slot instead of growing a queue. A dispatched mutation or flush always runs to completion even when its caller drops the future; only read jobs whose receiver is already gone are skipped. open and open_home stay synchronous and validate eagerly on the caller thread before moving the journal to its worker; open_async and open_home_async open, validate and save a reconstructed index on the worker. StateStore::flush saves the index behind earlier jobs. Dropping the handle never blocks: the worker drains dispatched jobs, saves the index the same way and exits. close awaits that exit. Measure cancellation, reopen and per-call overhead when changing the worker; output/nonblocking-store-20261017 holds the consumer that measured heartbeat gaps on a current-thread runtime.

JsonlStore::compact is an explicit asynchronous SDK maintenance operation on the owning worker. It keeps all valid acquisition and revision entries, each state's current payload, and every unique immutable record, and drops superseded entries, seal lines and unparseable fragments. Older state payloads become empty strings, so the compacted journal remains readable by older binaries and historical checkpoints remain resolvable. It never prunes records by guessing reachability from an opaque payload. The compactor first appends a seal line naming its process id; entries after the first unreleased seal are superseded, so the rewrite covers exactly the journal before the seal. Writers that find their entries after a seal wait for the replacement and retry on it. The wait polls from 1 ms, doubling to at most 100 ms: the 12.6 GB production snapshot took 31.4 s to compact, so the cap adds at most about 0.3% to a full wait while keeping a waiting writer to ten checks per second. A compactor that loses the seal race waits for the other compaction and seals again. If the sealing process has exited without publishing, the next writer appends an unseal line and continues. Liveness comes from the process id, so processes sharing a journal must share a process id namespace; a reused id only prolongs the wait. Compaction reports before_bytes as the bytes before its seal, along with after_bytes and records_removed. When the rewrite would not shrink those bytes it unseals instead of publishing and reports them unchanged. Startup, close and ordinary flush do not initiate compaction.

A compacted journal's first acquire entry carries nanocode_journal, the original journal identity, which later compactions retain. Upgraded handles reopen a replacement only when this lineage matches and reset their offsets, caches and seal state. Unrelated replacements remain errors. Do not publish a stale sidecar for a replaced inode, and do not publish one while the journal is sealed. Compaction preserves file permissions, synchronizes the replacement before atomic publication and the parent directory before acknowledging it, reopens it in append mode, and rejects hard-linked journals because replacing one name would split their writers. Cancellation after dispatch does not abandon compaction.

The journal owns temporary filename prefixes formed from its filename followed by .compact- or .index- and the creating process id. At open and before compaction, remove regular files in those namespaces whose process has exited. Leave files of running processes and unnamed legacy temporaries in place, because no lock can prove they are abandoned. This bounds storage left by a killed compactor or index writer to work awaiting the next open.
