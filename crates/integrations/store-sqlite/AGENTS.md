# SQLite store

SqliteStore is a handle to a dedicated worker thread that owns the rusqlite connection. Each StateStore call sends one complete job, so every IMMEDIATE acquisition or replacement transaction, including SQLite busy-timeout waits on another writer, runs and commits on the worker and never on an asynchronous runtime worker. The job channel holds one command: StateStore takes &mut self, so a handle has at most one live call, and the slot lets that caller queue its next command behind a cancelled predecessor that is still running; further sends wait for the slot instead of growing a queue. A dispatched acquisition, replacement or flush always runs to completion even when its caller drops the future; only read jobs whose receiver is already gone are skipped. flush waits behind earlier jobs because commits are already durable. acquire_with and companion acquisition keep the trait's sequential fallback. open, open_home and from_connection stay synchronous and create and validate the schema on the caller thread before moving the connection to its worker; open_async, open_home_async and from_connection_async do that work on the worker. Dropping the handle never blocks: the worker drains dispatched jobs, closes the connection and exits. close awaits that connection close and reports its error.

Measure heartbeat gaps on a current-thread runtime while another process holds an IMMEDIATE transaction, together with cancellation, fencing, reopen and per-call overhead, when changing the worker. output/nonblocking-sqlite-20261017 holds the consumer that measured a 1 s external write hold as 1087-1096 ms without heartbeats before and 2.7-4.9 ms maximum heartbeat gaps after, with a 200 ms timeout cancelling at 201-202 ms while the write still committed, at 12-27 us added per uncontended read.
